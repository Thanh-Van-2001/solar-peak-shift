Utility Solar Is Moving the Net-Load Peak: Evidence from 42 U.S. Balancing Authorities, 2019--2026

Utility-scale solar moves the hour at which net load (demand minus solar and wind) peaks into the evening, when dispatchable capacity, storage and imports must ramp up. Using hourly EIA-930 data for 42 U.S. balancing authorities (BAs) and a pre-specified analysis plan, we compare the summers of 2019–2020 with those of 2025–2026. In the 18 BAs where utility solar supplied at least 5% of 2025 demand, the median hour of the daily net-load peak moved later in all 17 BAs with a change (Holm p < 0.001), by a median of 1 hour per BA (mean 1.6). The share of days with the net-load peak at hour ending 20 or later rose from 8% to 65%, the gap to the demand peak widened in 13 of 14 BAs, and the shift exceeded that of low-solar BAs (Holm p = 0.001). The shift comes from solar rather than wind, and it survives month stratification, a comparison of 2019 with 2025 and the exclusion of BAs with suspect solar timestamps. The median three-hour ramp into the net-load peak grew 1.5-fold per BA. The operators' published demand forecast was not measurably less accurate at the net-load peak hour than at the demand peak hour. Peak-oriented tariffs, demand response and forecast evaluation in solar-rich BAs should follow the net-load peak.

Keywords: Solar energy, net load, peak demand, load forecasting, EIA-930.
